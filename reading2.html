Reading Response 2: The New Turing Test

The New Turing Test made me think that maybe we are asking the wrong question about AI. We keep asking, “Was this made by a human or a machine?” as if humans never make boring things. A person can create something completely generic without any help from AI, which is both comforting and a little depressing.

I liked the three questions in the article: Did the work come from someone? Was it made for someone? Does it belong to a specific context? As a designer, these questions feel more useful to me than trying to catch AI like a detective. For example, I am using Iranian ornamental tiles in my poster project. The patterns are beautiful, but they are not there only to make the poster look pretty. They connect the design to my culture, memories, and point of view.

I agree with the author that the tool is not the whole problem. What matters is whether the final work feels specific, thoughtful, and human. AI can copy the appearance of almost anything, but having a real reason for making something is still more complicated. Apparently, being human now requires proof.
