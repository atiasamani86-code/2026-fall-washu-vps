The Coming Age of Calm Technology

Reading Response — Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown

What interested me most about this reading was the idea that technology does not always need our full attention. Weiser and Brown describe calm technology as something that can move between the center and the periphery of our attention. I found this interesting because so many digital products today seem designed to make us look at them as often as possible.

One example I really liked was the Dangling String, which communicates network activity through physical movement. Instead of showing numbers or complicated information on a screen, it allows people to notice changes in their environment naturally. It made me think about how information can be communicated without interrupting what we are doing.

I also connected this reading to my research on the Pomodoro Technique. A timer is supposed to help us focus, but constantly checking the remaining minutes can become another distraction. I wonder if a timer could communicate the passage of time through subtle visual changes rather than a countdown.

For me, the most important question is not how much information a technology can display, but how much attention it requires from us. As a designer, I want to explore how interfaces can support our activities while allowing us to remain focused on the world around us.
