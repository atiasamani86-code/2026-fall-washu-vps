Do Artifacts Have Ethics?

Reading Response — L. M. Sacasas (2014)

One thing I found interesting about this reading is the idea that technology is never completely neutral. We usually think of technology as a tool, and whether it is good or bad depends on how people use it. But Sacasas asks us to look beyond that. What if technology itself changes the way we think, behave, and interact with the world?

I think this is especially important in design. When we create something, we usually start with a problem and try to find a solution. We focus on making things easier, faster, or more convenient. But I don't think we always spend enough time thinking about what people might lose when we make something easier.

For example, social media was designed to connect people, and in many ways it does. But it also changes how we communicate, how we spend our time, and even how we see ourselves. Sometimes we become more focused on documenting an experience than actually experiencing it. This makes me wonder whether designing for convenience and connection always improves our lives.

Another part that stood out to me was the author's questions about what technology encourages us to notice and what it encourages us to ignore. I find these questions really powerful because design is always making choices about attention. What we make visible, what we hide, and what we make easy or difficult can influence people's behavior without them even realizing it.

At the same time, I don't think this means technology is necessarily bad or that designers can predict every consequence. I think it means we need to be more aware of our responsibility. A design can solve one problem while creating another, and sometimes those consequences only become visible after people start using it.

For me, the most important takeaway is that ethical design is not just about avoiding harm or protecting people's data. It is also about understanding how our designs shape everyday life, human relationships, and people's choices.

The question I am left with is: Are we designing only for what people need right now, or are we also thinking about how our designs might change their lives over time?
